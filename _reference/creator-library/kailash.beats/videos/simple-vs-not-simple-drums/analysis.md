## Layout (one frame does it all)
- **Top ~36%:** a camera panel, landscape-cropped. Hook: talking head to camera with a mic. Body: B-roll of him in
  headphones, head down, nodding at the desk, dark moody room.
- **Bottom ~64%:** three stacked **segments**, each one element's FL piano roll (Hi Hats / 808 / Counter Snare),
  with thin black gaps between them. Each segment has a big centred white label naming the element.
- The segments are **static pages**: the whole section's pattern is visible at once, with no scrolling and no
  follow. FL's green playhead **sweeps left to right across all three in lockstep** (same x, ~75 px/s on a
  1320 px wide screen, so one pass ≈ 15.5 s = the whole section).
- The viewer sees the whole groove at once and hears each note as the playhead crosses it. That is the hook of
  the format: it's satisfying and educational.

## Takeaways for Jack's version
- Very long holds: each section is one full playhead pass. Cuts happen only at section changes, through a soft
  light-leak flash (the registry has `light-leak` / `flash-through-white`).
- For samples: the segments could be the sample's layers (e.g. chords / counter-melody / bass / arp). For beats:
  drums vs 808 vs melody, or a "simple vs not simple" / "before vs after" contrast.
- **Text:** the labels and the section titles are part of the format. This conflicts with Jack's standing
  "no on-screen text" rule for J Sewell edits; ask him per style.
- **Footage needed:** each element's piano roll recorded with **follow / scrolling OFF**, zoomed out so the whole
  section (e.g. 4 or 8 bars) fits on one page, playhead sweeping across. Same zoom and the same page start for
  every element so the playheads line up. Plus a face / B-roll clip of him vibing in headphones for the top.
